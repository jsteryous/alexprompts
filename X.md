# X (@steryously) — the posting spine

What goes on X, what does not, and why. This is the positioning layer for one channel, so it
sits beside `BRAND.md` and follows the same rule: it frames the work, it never bends the
analysis. The engines' research method in `scripts/*/routine/` stays as is.

Written July 29, 2026. Supersedes nothing; it writes down the thesis settled July 17, 2026
(memory `x-positioning-deep-tech`).

## The thesis

> **Tech needs land, power, and water. I read the records to see who is paying for it in
> South Carolina.**

That sentence is the whole channel. It came out of the NorthMark data-center piece and it is
the only positioning line that serves both of Alex's goals with the same post.

**The beat is infrastructure capital, not real estate.** Data centers, the grid and
interconnect queues, water withdrawal, fiber routes, the port, automation capex at the big
manufacturers, and who is buying the land underneath all of it. Deeds, fee-in-lieu-of-tax
agreements, utility filings, and county records are how the beat is *reported*. They are the
microscope, not the subject. This distinction is the entire difference between reading as a
technical analyst and reading as a realtor.

## Why this resolves the tech-sales tension

The worry that a Greenville real-estate feed competes with a deep-tech sales reputation is
correct, and the fix is not to post less about South Carolina. It is to be right about what
the subject is.

1. **To a hiring manager**, someone who can read a utility filing and do power math is a peer
   who understands the buyer, not a candidate claiming to be technical. The credential emerges
   from the work. Never lead with the title "tech sales professional."
2. **To the ecosystem**, the people who buy deep-tech products and the people who hire
   deep-tech salespeople are the same room: utility planners, site-selection consultants,
   data-center developers, plant operations leaders, economic development staff. Competent
   records analysis is what gets them to follow, reply, and take a DM.
3. **To the referral funnel**, an executive relocating into that same ecosystem is exactly the
   lead `/find-a-pro` wants.

So X is not a portfolio a hiring manager might audit some day. It is a prospecting motion that
happens to leave a portfolio behind. Optimize for the reply, not the impression count.

**Do not become a national deep-tech commentator.** A thousand accounts do national AI
commentary better and earlier. South Carolina plus infrastructure plus primary documents is the
defensible position, and it is defensible precisely because it is narrow and requires work.

## The filter

Before anything posts, one test:

> **Does this show me reading a primary source, and does it land on a number or an honest
> trade-off rather than an opinion?**

If someone who only read the newspaper could have written it, it is off brand. If it is a take
with no document behind it, it is off brand. Applied honestly this filter does more than any
content calendar.

## The mix

| Share | Type | Serves |
|---|---|---|
| ~60% | SC infrastructure from records | the whole thesis |
| ~25% | National tech or capital news, read through to South Carolina | reach, and legibility to non-locals |
| ~15% | Building in public (the engines, the tools, what broke) | tech-sales credibility directly |

The 25% is how the account stays visible to people who do not care about South Carolina yet.
It is a read-through, never a pure national take: the national item is the hook and the SC
consequence is the post.

### What does not go on X

- **Median-price and market-stat posts.** They serve the referral funnel and belong in the
  Upstate Brief, on Nextdoor, and on Facebook, where actual buyers are. On X they pull the
  account toward realtor, which is the exact drift this document exists to stop.
- **Sales as a craft.** No posts about discovery calls, quota, pipeline, or how to sell. The
  competence has to be visible in the analysis instead.
- **Listings, open houses, "just closed," anything transactional.**
- **Engagement bait**, rage-quoting, and dunking on local officials. The stance is pro-growth
  and accountability-flavored, never anti-development (memory `pro-growth-editorial-stance`).
  Be hard on the standard, not on the people.

## The supply problem, stated plainly

As of July 29, 2026, fifteen posts are published on the site: eleven evergreen real-estate
guides, three Upstate Briefs, and **one** Greenville Works infrastructure piece (the NorthMark
data center, July 4). Every engine emits one `## X` block per piece, so an X feed sourced from
the engines is about 93% real estate by construction. That is the mechanical reason staying on
brand feels like a fight.

Two fixes, in order of leverage:

1. **Post standalone, not derivative.** The record-find format below needs no essay behind it
   and takes about ten minutes. Three of those a week is a real feed. Link posts are the
   weakest format on X anyway, so rationing brand voice to whatever the essay pipeline produced
   is doubly wrong.
2. **Scale the Greenville Works cadence.** It is set to about monthly because it was scoped as
   the low-priority credibility track. It is now also the only engine that manufactures X
   material. Every two weeks first; weekly only if review and posting keep up.

## Post formats

Figures below are drawn from published pieces and are shapes, not evergreen facts. Re-verify
any number at its primary source before posting it.

### 1. The record find (flagship, target three a week)

One document, one number, one implication. No link required, no article needed. This is the
format that makes the account.

> The NorthMark data center on Spartanburg's old Kohler site would generate more than 450 MW on
> site by burning gas. That is not a grid connection, it is a private power plant with a server
> farm attached. The jobs and tax figures in the public record do not agree with each other.

Why it works: the number is specific, the translation is one sentence a non-expert gets, and it
ends on a discrepancy rather than a verdict. Do not editorialize past what the document says.

### 2. National story, local read-through

> [National capex or siting headline.] Every one of those campuses needs an interconnect, and
> [utility]'s queue in the Upstate is already carrying [figure, sourced]. Here is what decides
> whether the next one lands in South Carolina.

The national item earns the impression. The SC read-through earns the follow.

### 3. The mechanism explainer

How a real system actually works, in three or four sentences, with no news hook. Evergreen,
repostable, and the format most likely to make an insider follow because it proves you know the
constraints. Site selection, interconnect queues, water withdrawal permits, fee-in-lieu-of-tax
mechanics, why a fab needs a certain water quality.

### 4. Building in public

> My site publishes itself. A scheduled agent researches one South Carolina infrastructure
> story, writes it, checks its own figures against the primary sources, and leaves a draft in my
> review queue. I approve it or kill it. Built solo, runs inside free tiers.

Frame every technical detail as evidence of a trait, never a brag list, the same rule
`BRAND.md` sets for `/about`. Owning the engines end to end shows ownership; free tiers show
respect for unit economics.

### 5. The steelman

Take the strongest case against your own read and give it a fair hearing in public, then say
what would change your mind. This is the house method (`pro-growth-editorial-stance`) and on X
it is also the most credible thing a person can do, because almost nobody does it.

## Replies are the job

For the first ninety days, replies matter more than posts. A useful reply with a number in it,
left under someone credible, outperforms a good post with no audience.

Reply where the ecosystem is: utility and grid analysts, data-center and site-selection trade
accounts, SC economic development staff, industrial operations people, and the reporters who
cover Upstate development. Add a fact or a document they did not have. Never pitch, never
mention the referral business, and let the profile do the explaining.

## Voice on X

House style from the root `CLAUDE.md` still applies: no em dashes or en dashes, no sentence
fragments, complete flowing sentences, plain English, no hype, sparing colons.

Three things differ from site copy:

- **First person, and direct.** X is Alex talking, closer to the Greenville Works voice than to
  the evergreen guides.
- **Clippable by construction.** The clippable-lead rule (July 27, 2026) is native here: lead
  with the plain-language meaning, then stack the two or three figures that earn it, keep the
  source tag inside the post. Compress the prose, never the evidence. A post trimmed of its
  figures to look punchy is a failure.
- **A question beats a verdict**, but only a question you genuinely do not know the answer to.

## Bio

Lead with the beat, not the title.

- "I read South Carolina's records to find out who pays for the power, land, and water that AI
  needs." Then the site link.
- Alternate: "Data centers, the grid, and the port. What the public record says about who is
  paying for South Carolina's build-out."

## Keeping the engines in sync

The engines emit `## X` blocks that this document governs:

- `scripts/tech/routine/pass3_writer.md` — one post plus an optional 2 to 4 post thread. This is
  the only engine producing true infrastructure material, so it should always emit the full
  thread, not the single post.
- `scripts/briefing/routine/pass2_writer.md` — currently one link post carrying the week's best
  stat. Under the mix above, a market stat is the weakest thing the Brief has. It should emit
  two or three standalone posts instead, and prefer any development, rates-policy, or
  infrastructure item over the price line.
- `scripts/greenville/routine/pass_evergreen.md` — evergreen guides are the referral engine, not
  the X engine. Its `## X` output usually belongs on Nextdoor and Facebook, which is what the
  `## CLIPS` NEIGHBOR clip is already for.
