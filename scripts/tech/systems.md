# South Carolina systems inventory

The durable list of real, engineered systems in South Carolina that a Greenville Works piece can
take apart. This file is the raw material for `routine/pass0_scout.md`, which crosses these systems
against the five question shapes to generate its own topics. It is deliberately NOT a topic queue:
it does not drain, because one system supports many different questions depending on which shape
you point at it. Oconee alone is worth four or five separate pieces.

**Every figure below is a LEAD, not a fact.** They are here so the scout knows a system is
interesting and roughly how big it is, not so anyone can publish them. The scout confirms a spec
before it ships a question, and the researcher re-confirms every load-bearing number against a
primary source. Entries marked `[verify]` are ones nobody in this repo has checked yet.

Add a system when you notice one. Do not remove a system after a piece runs; a covered system is
still available to a different shape, and `## done` in `topics.md` is what prevents repeats.

## Generation and the grid

- **Oconee Nuclear Station** (Oconee County, Duke). 2,538 MW, three pressurized-water reactors
  online 1973 and 1974. NRC renewed the licenses in spring 2025 to an 80-year life ending 2053 and
  2054. Cooled by Lake Keowee. The single largest engineered object in the Upstate.
- **Bad Creek Pumped Storage** (Oconee County, Duke). 1,680 MW after a 320 MW uprate completed
  April 2024. Pumps water uphill from Lake Jocassee to store energy. The state's only large battery,
  and it is made of water and gravity.
- **Keowee-Toxaway project** (Duke). The Jocassee and Keowee reservoir system that makes both Oconee
  and Bad Creek possible. One water system, two very different machines. `[verify]` license terms.
- **V.C. Summer** (Fairfield County, Jenkinsville). Unit 1 operating under Dominion Energy South
  Carolina `[verify]` capacity. Units 2 and 3 abandoned July 2017 after roughly $9 billion; Santee
  Cooper's board has approved a Brookfield proposal to finish the two AP1000s, about 2,200 MWe and
  roughly $2.7 billion, with a go/no-go by the first half of 2028 and a stated claim of no ratepayer
  funding. The partially built structures have stood exposed since 2017.
- **Catawba Nuclear Station** (York County, Duke). Two units. `[verify]` capacity and license dates.
- **H.B. Robinson** (Darlington County, Duke). One unit, among the oldest operating in the country.
  `[verify]`.
- **Duke's 2025 Carolinas Resource Plan.** Proposes about 9.7 GW of new gas by 2033, five
  combined-cycle units and seven combustion turbines. Duke describes a roughly $103 billion capital
  program across 2026 to 2030, about 65 percent aimed at grid and generation, at a 9.99 percent
  authorized return.
- **Santee Cooper.** The state-owned utility. Cross and Winyah generating stations, the Lake Moultrie
  and Lake Marion system, and the Base Load Review Act history. `[verify]` current fleet.
- **The interconnection queue.** Median time from request to operation nationally was about five
  years for plants reaching service in 2023, per Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. This is the
  real constraint on where a large load can plug in, more than land or water.
- **Utility-scale solar.** Widespread in the Midlands and Lowcountry; Act 236 and Act 62 set the
  net-metering and successor-tariff rules that govern the rooftop side. `[verify]` current tariff.
- **Rooftop solar.** Conspicuously rare relative to the utility-scale build. The gap is the story.

## Nuclear and defense

- **Savannah River Site** (Aiken County, DOE). The nation's tritium production and processing
  site, plus the plutonium pit mission that replaced the cancelled MOX facility. `[verify]` current
  mission scope and headcount. Aiken County's economy is downstream of a federal nuclear complex.
- **Lockheed Martin Greenville** (SCTAC). The F-16 production line. SCTAC itself is a 2,600-acre
  business park on the old Donaldson air base.
- **Boeing North Charleston.** 787 final assembly and delivery since 2009.
- **SC Defense.** Statewide initiative launched at the 2026 SC Competes Spring Summit, folding
  together the SC Logistics, SC Tech, and SC Aerospace clusters.

## Manufacturing and automation

- **BMW Plant Spartanburg.** X-series assembly and battery work. Among the largest US vehicle
  exporters by value, shipping through Charleston. `[verify]` current volumes, headcount, and
  automation levels. Opened 1994, which gives a clean 30-year automation baseline.
- **Michelin.** Multiple South Carolina plants plus the North American headquarters in Greenville.
- **Scout Motors** (Blythewood, Richland County). New EV plant. `[verify]` status and capacity.
- **Nucor Berkeley.** Electric-arc-furnace steel, which is itself a large electrical load.
- **Bosch** (Anderson and Charleston). `[verify]`.
- **Milliken** (Spartanburg). Textiles and materials research.
- **Trane Technologies** (Columbia). Builds commercial chillers and HVAC systems. The product is
  energy efficiency, which makes it a rare case where a South Carolina plant's output is measured in
  electricity NOT consumed elsewhere. `[verify]` plant scope and whether any SC-specific savings
  figure is published anywhere.
- **Edgewater Automation** (Spartanburg). Custom automation systems for automotive and defense.
- **Integrion.** Battery test and assembly systems for EV and consumer products. `[verify]`.
- **SETi** (Columbia). Deep-UV LED manufacturer. Genuine deep tech, almost no local coverage.
- **CeramTec.** Advanced ceramics and hermetic components for lasers, semiconductors, defense.

## Research and test infrastructure

- **Clemson's energy testing complex, North Charleston.** Houses what is described as the world's
  largest wind turbine drivetrain test facility, with large-scale test rigs, plus a grid simulator
  for hardware-in-the-loop testing. `[verify]` current capacity, ownership, and naming. South
  Carolina tests the world's wind turbines and installs almost none of them.
- **Clemson CU-ICAR** (Greenville). Automotive research campus.
- **University research capacity.** Clemson, USC, MUSC. `[verify]` before using any of it.

## Port, rail, and logistics

- **Port of Charleston** (SC Ports Authority). Hugh K. Leatherman Terminal, the harbor deepening
  project, and the container volumes that drive Upstate warehousing demand. `[verify]` depth and
  current volumes.
- **Inland Port Greer** and **Inland Port Dillon.** Rail shuttles that put the Upstate a night's
  train ride from the wharf, and the reason distribution land near Greer is priced the way it is.
- **I-85 and I-26 corridors** (SCDOT). The physical spine that the manufacturing lattice sits on.

## Water, sewer, and land

- **Greenville Water.** Table Rock and Poinsett reservoirs plus the Lake Keowee intake. The real
  question is headroom in millions of gallons per day against data center and housing demand.
  `[verify]` capacity and current draw.
- **ReWa.** Upstate wastewater treatment capacity, which quietly decides where subdivisions can go.
- **Charleston flooding and drainage.** Sea level, the tunnel system, and the engineering response.
  `[verify]`.

## Connectivity

- **Fiber builds.** Upcountry Fiber (the Blue Ridge and West Carolina cooperative venture), AT&T,
  Spectrum, plus the federal BEAD allocation to South Carolina. `[verify]` award amounts and current
  build status. The last-mile economics are why one street gets wired and the next waits.

## Data centers and the rules being written around them

- **The Upstate and Midlands campuses.** NorthMark's roughly $2.8 billion proposal at the former
  Kohler plant in Spartanburg (already covered), Google's long-running Berkeley County campus, and
  newer filings. `[verify]` each before use.
- **The 2026 rejection wave.** Spartanburg County rejected a $3 billion data center at Tyger River
  Industrial Park in February 2026 and voted 6-0 to draft a one-year moratorium. Chester County
  passed a six-month moratorium unanimously. Orangeburg advanced one on July 23. Sumter moved toward
  a pause covering solar, data centers, and battery sites after rejecting a 1,700-acre solar farm.
  Berkeley County is moving the other way, advancing a plan to lift the Cross moratorium for a
  1,500-acre solar farm.
- **The bills.** H5215, the Ratepayer Protection and Large Load Customer Infrastructure
  Accountability Act, which would give preferential rate-agreement treatment to data centers with a
  power usage effectiveness below 1.3. S867, the Data Center Development Act, favorably reported out
  of Senate committee April 28, 2026. H5286, which would bar any new data center approval statewide
  until January 1, 2028. The 2025 Energy Security Act (H3309) sits underneath all of them.
- **The PSC docket.** Sierra Club settlements require the South Carolina Public Service Commission to
  open a docket on large-load tariffs. `[verify]` docket number and status.
