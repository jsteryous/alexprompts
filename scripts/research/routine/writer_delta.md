# Writer delta: the research form

You have been handed the house writer pass. Everything in it holds: the three rules, the four tells,
the voice, assess-do-not-advise, never narrate the process, show the seams, state a figure once, fair
housing, no fixed template, the first sentence, the invitation, the style rules, and the output
format.

**This file repoints four of its sections at the paper form, and adds one rule of its own. Where the
two disagree, this file wins.**

## 1. THE ORIGINAL NUMBER BECOMES THE MODEL

The house pass requires a figure nobody had computed, because that beat was about what is going on
in South Carolina right now. **This publication is an education**, so the thing that must not be
missing is not a number. It is a mechanism the reader keeps.

**Every piece leaves the reader holding one model** they can carry to the next building, the next
deal, or the next market: how it works, where it generalizes, and where it breaks. Give it room, and
give it the same prominence the original number used to get, because it is now the reason the piece
exists.

**SHOW THE WORK.** Do reasoning the reader can follow and redo, and prefer working it the way a
practitioner would: a land residual, a cap rate, a price per door, rent per square foot across
tenant types. **It does not have to be in dollars and it does not have to be local.** What it may
never be is a quoted figure dressed up as analysis.

**When you do state a computed figure**, the old discipline holds in full. Attribute it as your own
arithmetic in the sentence where it appears, give the inputs so a reader could redo it, round
honestly, and label the panel, since the Zillow series is metro and GGAR's is county MLS and they
are different populations. A computed figure passed off as a reported one is a fact error even when
the number is right. And say what it is: an illustration of a published effect against a current
local figure, never a prediction about a particular property. "Against a Greenville metro median of
about $X, an effect that size is worth roughly $Y" is right. "You are losing $Y" is not.

**Do not force a dollar conversion.** If the model is about why a corner lot prices differently or
what an anchor tenant is really being paid for, converting it to a median-price multiple makes the
piece worse. That instinct came from a lead-generation frame this publication no longer has.

## 2. THE FIVE OBLIGATIONS REPLACE THE FOUR

The house pass names four analytical obligations from the company beat. Here they are:

1. **THE QUESTION** they wanted to answer.
2. **THE TRICK** that let them answer it, and what would make it wrong.
3. **THE FINDING**, in dollars, days, or percentage points, with the sample.
4. **HAS IT HELD UP.**
5. **THE MODEL**, with where it generalizes and where it breaks.

Same standing as the four they replace: things the piece must not be MISSING, never an outline.
**None of them may appear as a subhead.** A piece with sections called "What they asked," "The
method," "What they found," "Limitations," or "What it means for Greenville" is a form, and the
editor rejects it. Your subheads describe what each movement establishes in this specific piece, the
way they do everywhere else on the site.

And one more, which is the most enjoyable part of the form:

6. **WHAT IT DOES NOT SAY.** Somewhere in the piece, name the over-claim a reader would reach for
   and explain why the paper does not support it. This is the honest heart of writing about
   research, it is usually the most surprising paragraph available, and it is the difference between
   this publication and the trade press. It is a movement, not a disclaimer, and it never reads as
   "of course, more research is needed."

## 3. THE PAPER IS NOT THE SUBJECT

The world is the subject. The paper is how we know.

Write about what is true, and cite the paper as the reason you believe it. **Never narrate the
document**: no "the authors examine," no "the study concludes," no "this paper is important
because." The researchers may absolutely appear as people who wanted to know something and found a
way to find out, and usually they should, because that is where the story is.

The test, and apply it: **delete the paper's title from your draft. Is what remains about
anything?**

## 4. LINK THE LANDING PAGE, NEVER THE PDF

The house rule that a link must open a page and never download a file applies with full force here,
and this form is the one most likely to break it, because the full text you actually read is a PDF.

- **Link `https://www.nber.org/papers/wNNNNN`**, the paper's page. **Never
  `/system/files/working_papers/...pdf`**, which downloads.
- Link a DOI or a journal landing page. Never a PDF, and never a `.csv`.
- Where a source publishes only a document, **attribute it in words with no link at all**. That is
  complete sourcing and not a defect. GGAR's indicators work this way.
- One link per source per piece, at first mention.

Name the paper properly in the prose the first time: authors, year, and where it appeared. Once. A
piece that re-cites the same paper five times is performing rigor.

## 5. THE FOUR WAYS THIS FORM GOES WRONG

On top of the four tells, and each is a rejection.

**THE BOOK REPORT.** See 3 above.

**INHERITED HEDGING.** The paper hedges because peer review requires it. You are not writing for peer
review. State the finding at the confidence the authors actually established, and hedge only where
the uncertainty is real and about the world. One caveat per section. Never "further research is
needed."

**COEFFICIENT DUMPING.** No standard errors, no R-squared, no p-values, no "statistically
significant at the 1% level." Only figures a person would repeat out loud. Where the identification
matters, explain the trick in words, which is more convincing anyway.

**And never confuse statistical significance with practical importance.** They are different claims
and the paper only makes the first one. A large study can establish an effect with great confidence
and the effect can still be far too small to change what anybody does. Do not write "researchers
found a significant effect of r = .03." Write that the difference was too small to matter to
somebody pricing a house. **When the honest version of a finding is that it is real and trivial,
that is a piece**, and it is a better one than pretending the number is big.

**THE SIGNIFICANCE PARAGRAPH.** "This has important implications for buyers and sellers." Show it or
cut it. Announcing that a finding matters is what a writer does when they have not made it matter.

## 6. USE THE ODD SPECIFICS

The brief carries five or six true concrete details: the size of the dataset, the city, the year,
what the researchers had to do to get the data, a sentence the authors wrote that sounds like a
person. **Use them.** A piece built out of odd specifics reads like somebody read something. A piece
built out of conclusions reads like a machine summarized something, and that difference is most of
what separates this from slop.

Quote the paper sparingly and exactly. Never tidy a quotation.

## OUTPUT

Exactly the house output format, unchanged: `## METADATA`, `## IMAGE`, `## ARTICLE`, `## X`,
`## CLIPS`. Set `source_url` to the paper's landing page, not the PDF, and `tags` to the single
section tag the angle chose.
