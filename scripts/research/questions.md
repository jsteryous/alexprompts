# What Alex wants to know

**This is Alex's file and it outranks the canon.** The scout reads it first. A paper that answers
a question sitting here beats a more-cited paper that answers one nobody asked, because the whole
engine exists so that he learns something, and he is the first reader.

Add a line whenever you get curious about something. It does not have to be well formed, and it
does not have to have a paper attached. Finding the paper is the engine's job.

Format: one question per bullet. Mark it `[done: slug]` once a piece has answered it, rather than
deleting it, so the file stays a record of what has been learned.

---

## Seeded August 27, 2026, from Alex's own framing

He asked for three things: the history of real estate, the data behind what actually drives
decisions, and the papers that asked a great question. These are the starting set.

### Where the institutions came from

- Why is the standard American mortgage thirty years long and fixed? Nothing about that is
  natural, and it was invented in a specific decade for specific reasons.
- Why is a commission around 5 or 6 percent, split four ways, and why has that number been so
  stubborn when almost every other brokered fee collapsed?
- Where did the MLS come from, what problem was it built to solve, and who did it solve it for?
- Why does a closing take a month and involve that many parties? Which of those steps exist for a
  real reason and which are residue?
- Where did zoning come from, and how did a 1926 Supreme Court case end up drawing setback lines in
  Greenville County?
- Greenville's mill villages were sold off to the workers who lived in them. What did that do to
  the housing stock that is still standing?

### What actually drives a decision

- Does the list price you choose change what the house sells for, or only how long it takes?
- What does a day on market actually signal to a buyer, and when does it start costing money?
- Do staging, photography, and listing copy move price, or do they move speed?
- How much of a school-zone premium is the school and how much is the neighbors?
- What does the evidence say a buyer's agent is actually worth to a buyer?
- Does anything about a bidding war behave the way people think it does?

### Urban economics, the foundation layer

Added August 27, 2026, in Alex's words: he wants "a deep understanding of real estate, how the
market works, what developers look for, which businesses are the best tenants, which income
producing assets are the best, which home styles are the best and worst investments, urban
economics."

- Why does the same house cost three times more in one metro than another? How much of that is
  geography, how much is regulation, and how much is just demand?
- Why is land worth what it is at a given distance from downtown, and what actually sets that
  gradient?
- Is Greenville's price run a cycle that reverts, or a permanent step up of the kind a handful of
  cities made?
- What does a metro have to do to make housing supply respond to demand, and does anywhere actually
  do it?

### What developers look for

- How does a developer actually price land? What is the residual, and what goes into it?
- Why do valuable lots sit empty for years? What is waiting worth?
- What is an entitlement actually worth, and who captures that value?
- Which risks does a developer get paid to take, and which ones do they just eat?

### Tenants and income-producing assets

- Which businesses are genuinely good tenants, and what is credit quality worth in rent?
- Why does an anchor tenant pay so much less per square foot than the shop next to it, and is that
  the tenant winning or the landlord being rational?
- What is each property type's return actually compensating you for? Where does the yield on
  self-storage or industrial come from?
- Why do office markets overbuild on such a reliable cycle, and does the same thing happen to
  industrial and multifamily?
- What does a cap rate really encode, and why does a small move in it swamp a good year of rent
  growth?

### Which houses are good and bad investments

- Which home styles, vintages, and layouts hold value, and which quietly do not?
- How fast does a house actually depreciate if you do not maintain it, and what does maintenance
  really return?
- Does renovation pay? Which kinds, and under what conditions?
- What does filtering do over decades, and is today's new construction tomorrow's affordable
  housing?
- How much of a lot's value is the lot, and when does the building become a liability?

### Papers that asked a great question

- Which real estate findings that everyone repeats have actually failed to replicate?
- What is the best natural experiment anyone has ever found in housing, and what did it show?
- What does the research on salespeople say separates the top decile from the median, and does any
  of it transfer to agents?
- Where does the academic literature flatly contradict what the industry teaches in training?

## Notes on using this file

A question here can be answered by a paper, by a primary document, or by both. When a question
turns out to have no reachable evidence behind it, say so in `read.md` and leave the question in
place. An unanswerable question is worth knowing about, and it may become answerable later.
