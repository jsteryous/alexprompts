# The companies inventory

The scout's raw material. A durable list of South Carolina companies worth taking apart, which
it crosses against the six question shapes in `routine/pass0_scout.md` to generate candidates.

**This is an inventory, not a queue.** It does not get consumed. One company supports a
different piece under each shape, so a name stays on the list after it has been written about
and comes back years later from a different angle. That property is what keeps the generator
from draining, and it is why this file replaces `scripts/tech/systems.md` rather than copying
its habits.

**Every figure here is a LEAD, not a fact.** Nothing in this file has been verified. Headcounts
and revenues move, subsidiaries get sold, headquarters relocate. The researcher confirms
anything a piece relies on, from a filing or a primary source, and a number that cannot be
confirmed does not appear in the piece. Do not quote this file.

## What earns a place

A company belongs here when at least one of these is true:

- **Nobody can explain it.** Large or critical, and the average person in its own city cannot
  tell you what it sells. These are the best subjects on the list.
- **The obvious story is probably wrong.** The thing it is known for is not where the money is.
- **It sits on something physical and specific.** A plant, a port berth, a rail spur, a campus,
  a substation. Gives the piece a place and gives the real estate angle something real.
- **Its position is genuinely contested.** A model under pressure, a concentration risk, a
  technology shift arriving.

A company does NOT belong here just because it is big, or because it announced something. Size
without a puzzle is a directory entry.

## Entry format

```
### Company Name (City)
SECTOR: one line
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: and the ticker if public
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: the puzzle, in one or two sentences
LIKELY SHAPES: which of the six question shapes fit best
SOURCE FOOTHOLD: where the public record on this one starts
```

**PUBLIC/PRIVATE matters more than anything else in the entry**, because it decides whether the
ACCESS INDEPENDENCE gate can be cleared. A public company files, so the piece is always
writable from the record. A private one may only be reachable through permits, deeds, job
postings, incentive agreements, trade press, and litigation, which is often enough but has to
be checked before the run commits.

## Upstate

### ScanSource (Greenville)
SECTOR: technology distribution
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: public
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: one of the largest companies headquartered in Greenville and most people
who live there cannot say what it does. It manufactures nothing. The interesting part is that a
distributor's business is credit and logistics wearing a technology costume, and the company has
been pushing toward recurring services, which is a different business with different economics.
LIKELY SHAPES: the revenue question, the quiet giant, the break
SOURCE FOOTHOLD: SEC filings, which make this the safest possible first subject

### Milliken & Company (Spartanburg)
SECTOR: textiles, chemicals, performance materials, floor covering
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: private, and famously private
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: it is the company most associated with an industry South Carolina is
supposed to have lost. What it actually sells now is largely not textiles. The secrecy is itself
part of the story and part of the difficulty.
LIKELY SHAPES: the revenue question, why here, the quiet giant
SOURCE FOOTHOLD: patents, job postings, plant permits, trade press. Check ACCESS INDEPENDENCE
carefully before committing; a private company this closed may not clear it under every shape.

### BMW Manufacturing (Spartanburg County)
SECTOR: automotive
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: subsidiary of a public parent
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: the export math is the least understood thing about it, and the supplier
network it pulled into the Upstate is a bigger economic object than the plant.
LIKELY SHAPES: the constraint, why here, the break

### Michelin North America (Greenville)
SECTOR: tires, and considerably more than tires
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: subsidiary of a public French parent
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: multiple SC plants plus a North American headquarters in a city that did
not obviously earn one. The why-here has a real answer.
LIKELY SHAPES: why here, the constraint

### Techtronic Industries / TTI (Anderson)
SECTOR: power tools
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: subsidiary of a Hong Kong listed parent
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: the brands are household names and the company is not. Where the tools are
actually made, and how much of the SC operation is manufacturing versus distribution, is worth
establishing rather than assuming.
LIKELY SHAPES: the quiet giant, the revenue question

### GE Vernova gas power (Greenville)
SECTOR: gas turbines
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: public
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: heavy-duty turbines built in Greenville end up in power plants worldwide,
and demand for them is being reshaped by data centre load. A rare case where the local plant and
a national story are the same story.
LIKELY SHAPES: the constraint, the customer question

### Lockheed Martin (Greenville)
SECTOR: aerospace, F-16 production and sustainment
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: public
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: a single-customer business with a customer unlike any other, and a backlog
driven by foreign military sales rather than domestic orders.
LIKELY SHAPES: the customer question, the break

### Fluor (Greenville)
SECTOR: engineering and construction
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: public
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: enormous revenue, thin margins, and a project-risk model that has burned

LIKELY SHAPES: the revenue question, the break

### Southern First Bancshares and United Community Bank (Greenville)
SECTOR: banking
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: public
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: the readership IS banking, and a bank's loan book is the most honest

## Harvesting new entries

The scout must be able to add to this list, or the inventory becomes the ceiling. Four
reliable veins:

1. **The state's largest employers by county.** Most of these are known names. The interesting
   ones are the names that are not.
2. **Incentive and economic-development announcements.** Not to check whether the promise was
   kept, which is a different publication we decided against, but because an announcement names
   a company and says what it does, which is a lead.
3. **Public filings with a South Carolina address.** Anything filing with an SC principal office
   is by definition access-independent and therefore always writable.
4. **The buildings.** `src/data/commercialSales.json` holds Greenville County commercial deed
   records. A company that just bought a large building is a company doing something, and the
   deed gives the piece a physical anchor for free.

When adding an entry, fill every field. An entry without a SOURCE FOOTHOLD is a name, not a
lead, and it will waste a run when the scout picks it and the feasibility gate kills it.
