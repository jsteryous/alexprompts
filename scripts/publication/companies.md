# The companies inventory

The scout's raw material. A durable list of South Carolina companies worth taking apart, which
it crosses against the ten question shapes in `routine/pass0_scout.md` to generate candidates.

**This is an inventory, not a queue.** It does not get consumed. One company supports a
different piece under each shape, so a name stays on the list after it has been written about
and comes back years later from a different angle. That property is what keeps the generator
from draining, and it is why this file replaces `scripts/tech/systems.md` rather than copying
its habits.

**Every figure here is a LEAD, not a fact.** Nothing in this file has been verified. Headcounts
and revenues move, subsidiaries get sold, headquarters relocate. The researcher confirms
anything a piece relies on, from a filing or a primary source, and a number that cannot be
confirmed does not appear in the piece. Do not quote this file.

## What earns a place

> **CORRECTED August 14, 2026, and this is the most important change this file has had.** The
> first version selected for NOTABILITY: large, critical, unexplained, a household name with an
> industrial reality underneath. That produced a well-built teardown of ScanSource, a Fortune
> 1000 company, and Alex read it and said what a developer or a bank's portfolio manager actually
> needs is **the smaller companies, the ones looking to grow who might be in need of loans.**
>
> He is right and the reason is structural. ScanSource does not need a loan from Southern First.
> It is not anyone's tenant, it is not filling anyone's subdivision, and it is not going to lease
> 20,000 square feet next spring. **It is large, mature, and shrinking, which is three strikes
> against the only reader this publication has.** Picking famous companies is a media instinct.
> The reader's instinct is the opposite, because their business is with whoever is transacting.
>
> **The new principle: select for GROWING and CAPITAL-HUNGRY, not for big and unexplained.**

A company belongs here when the first test passes and at least one of the others does.

**THE FIRST TEST, and it is a gate.** Is this company expanding, borrowing, hiring, leasing, or
buying, and would a banker, a landlord, or a developer plausibly have a reason to want to
understand it? If the honest answer is no, it does not belong here no matter how interesting it
is. A mature company that is not transacting is a magazine subject, not a subject for this
publication.

Then at least one of:

- **It is growing and nobody has noticed yet.** The best entries on this list. Small enough that
  no one has written about it, big enough that its growth shows up in a public record.
- **It needs capital and the record shows it.** A UCC filing against equipment or receivables, a
  new mortgage on a building, an SBA loan, a lease at a size that implies a raise. This is the
  vein that most directly serves the reader.
- **It sits on something physical and specific.** A plant, a berth, a rail spur, a campus, a
  building it just bought. Gives the piece a place and the real estate angle something real.
- **The obvious story is probably wrong.** The thing it is known for is not where the money is.
  Still a good test, but on a company that passes the first gate.
- **Its position is genuinely contested.** A model under pressure, a concentration risk, a
  technology shift arriving.

**Size is a disqualifier more often than a qualifier now.** A company with an investor relations
page is already covered by people paid to cover it, and the marginal value of another explainer is
low. The company with twelve trucks and a new note against them is covered by nobody.

A public company is still the easiest to write, so it remains a reasonable ON-RAMP when the
inventory is thin, but it is no longer the preferred subject. If a run cannot find a growing
private company it can document, that is a signal to take a GROWTH MAP question instead.

## Entry format

```
### Company Name (City)
SECTOR: one line
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: and the ticker if public
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: the puzzle, in one or two sentences
LIKELY SHAPES: which of the ten question shapes fit best
SOURCE FOOTHOLD: where the public record on this one starts
```

**PUBLIC/PRIVATE matters more than anything else in the entry**, because it decides whether the
ACCESS INDEPENDENCE gate can be cleared. A public company files, so the piece is always
writable from the record. A private one may only be reachable through permits, deeds, job
postings, incentive agreements, trade press, and litigation, which is often enough but has to
be checked before the run commits.

## Upstate

### ScanSource (Greenville)
SECTOR: technology distribution
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: public
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: one of the largest companies headquartered in Greenville and most people
who live there cannot say what it does. It manufactures nothing. The interesting part is that a
distributor's business is credit and logistics wearing a technology costume, and the company has
been pushing toward recurring services, which is a different business with different economics.
LIKELY SHAPES: the revenue question, the quiet giant, the break
SOURCE FOOTHOLD: SEC filings, which make this the safest possible first subject

### Milliken & Company (Spartanburg)
SECTOR: textiles, chemicals, performance materials, floor covering
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: private, and famously private
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: it is the company most associated with an industry South Carolina is
supposed to have lost. What it actually sells now is largely not textiles. The secrecy is itself
part of the story and part of the difficulty.
LIKELY SHAPES: the revenue question, why here, the quiet giant
SOURCE FOOTHOLD: patents, job postings, plant permits, trade press. Check ACCESS INDEPENDENCE
carefully before committing; a private company this closed may not clear it under every shape.

### BMW Manufacturing (Spartanburg County)
SECTOR: automotive
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: subsidiary of a public parent
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: the export math is the least understood thing about it, and the supplier
network it pulled into the Upstate is a bigger economic object than the plant.
LIKELY SHAPES: the constraint, why here, the break

### Michelin North America (Greenville)
SECTOR: tires, and considerably more than tires
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: subsidiary of a public French parent
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: multiple SC plants plus a North American headquarters in a city that did
not obviously earn one. The why-here has a real answer.
LIKELY SHAPES: why here, the constraint

### Techtronic Industries / TTI (Anderson)
SECTOR: power tools
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: subsidiary of a Hong Kong listed parent
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: the brands are household names and the company is not. Where the tools are
actually made, and how much of the SC operation is manufacturing versus distribution, is worth
establishing rather than assuming.
LIKELY SHAPES: the quiet giant, the revenue question

### GE Vernova gas power (Greenville)
SECTOR: gas turbines
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: public
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: heavy-duty turbines built in Greenville end up in power plants worldwide,
and demand for them is being reshaped by data centre load. A rare case where the local plant and
a national story are the same story.
LIKELY SHAPES: the constraint, the customer question

### Lockheed Martin (Greenville)
SECTOR: aerospace, F-16 production and sustainment
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: public
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: a single-customer business with a customer unlike any other, and a backlog
driven by foreign military sales rather than domestic orders.
LIKELY SHAPES: the customer question, the break

### Fluor (Greenville)
SECTOR: engineering and construction
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: public
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: enormous revenue, thin margins, and a project-risk model that has burned
the company before. The mechanics of how an E&C firm actually earns are genuinely non-obvious.
LIKELY SHAPES: the revenue question, the break

### Southern First Bancshares and United Community Bank (Greenville)
SECTOR: banking
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: public
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: the readership IS banking, and a bank's loan book is the most honest
available portrait of what an economy is actually doing. Handle with the legal gate in view.
LIKELY SHAPES: the customer question, the constraint

## Midlands

### Nephron Pharmaceuticals (West Columbia)
SECTOR: generic injectables and inhalation drugs
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: private
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: sterile generic manufacturing is a brutal, low-margin, high-regulation
business that most US firms exited. Someone chose to build it here anyway.
LIKELY SHAPES: why here, the constraint, the break

### Scout Motors (Blythewood)
SECTOR: electric vehicles
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: subsidiary of Volkswagen
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: the incentive package is the largest in state history, the corporate
headquarters went to Charlotte, and no vehicle has been built yet. Rich, and correspondingly
sensitive: this one needs the legal gate applied strictly and every figure sourced to a filing.
LIKELY SHAPES: why here, the break

### AgFirst Farm Credit Bank (Columbia)
SECTOR: wholesale lending to agricultural credit associations
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: cooperative, files publicly
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: a multi-billion dollar bank in downtown Columbia that has no retail
customers and that almost nobody has heard of. Close to a perfect quiet giant.
LIKELY SHAPES: the quiet giant, the revenue question

### Santee Cooper (Moncks Corner)
SECTOR: state-owned electric and water utility
PUBLIC/PRIVATE: state-owned, files publicly
WHY IT EARNS A LOOK: owned by the state, carrying debt from an abandoned nuclear project, and
now being asked to serve new industrial load. Politically charged, so keep the analysis on the
balance sheet and off the politics.
LIKELY SHAPES: the constraint, the break

## Harvesting new entries

The scout must be able to add to this list, or the inventory becomes the ceiling. **Reordered
August 14, 2026 to put the growth-and-capital veins first**, since the largest-employer vein is
what produced the ScanSource miss.

1. **UCC filings.** The single best vein and the most under-used, at
   `https://ucconline.sc.gov/UCCFiling/UCCMainPage.aspx`. A UCC-1 financing statement gets filed
   when a lender takes a security interest, so this database is a continuously updated public
   record of **which South Carolina companies just borrowed money and what they pledged.** That is
   the reader's own question, asked of the whole state, searchable by debtor. A company that just
   pledged its equipment is a company doing something, and the filing names the lender too.
2. **Building permits and certificates of occupancy.** Who is building what, where, and at what
   declared value. Monthly, jurisdiction by jurisdiction. Commercial permits name the business.
3. **New business filings.** SC Secretary of State, continuous. Useful both for a genuinely new
   company and for identifying the human behind an LLC on a deed.
4. **Leases and deeds.** `src/data/commercialSales.json` holds Greenville County commercial deed
   records. A company that just bought or took a large building is a company doing something, and
   the deed gives the piece a physical anchor for free.
5. **Job postings at small companies.** A twenty-person firm posting six roles is growing fast in
   a way that no announcement will ever cover.
6. **FILOT ordinances and incentive agreements.** Mid-size expansions, with the terms attached.
   Not to check whether the promise was kept, which is a different publication we decided against,
   but because the ordinance names a company, an investment, and a jobs commitment.
7. **Public filings with a South Carolina address.** Always writable, because everything material
   is disclosed. Demoted to an on-ramp rather than a preferred vein; see the correction above.

When adding an entry, fill every field. An entry without a SOURCE FOOTHOLD is a name, not a
lead, and it will waste a run when the scout picks it and the feasibility gate kills it.

## A note on the existing entries

The entries below were written under the old notability principle and most of them are large,
mature companies. **They are not deleted**, because several still pass the first gate under a
different shape (Nephron is building, Scout Motors has not made a vehicle yet, the two banks are
lending), and because a big company remains a legitimate on-ramp when the inventory is thin. But
**the list is now unbalanced and the fix is to add small growing companies to it**, harvested
from veins 1 through 6, rather than to keep working down what is here.
